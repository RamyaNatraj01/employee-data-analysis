Executive Summary¶

Problem and data¶

This analysis looks for employee attrition patterns that may help prioritize further retention efforts. The cleaned IBM HR Analytics dataset contains 1,470 employee records and 35 columns. A total of 237 employees are marked as having left, an overall attrition rate of 16.1%.

Three key insights¶

  1. Attrition is higher among employees with shorter tenure: 34.9% for employees with 0-1 years at the company, compared with 8.1% for employees with 11 or more years.
  2. Attrition differs by job role. The rate is 39.8% for Sales Representatives and 23.9% for Laboratory Technicians, compared with 16.1% overall.
  3. Employees marked as attrition 'Yes' have lower average monthly income (4,787 dataset units) and shorter average tenure (5.13 years) than employees marked 'No' (6,833 units and 7.37 years). These are associations, not evidence that income or tenure causes attrition.

Two recommendations¶

  1. Pilot structured onboarding and regular check-ins during the first year, then measure whether retention changes.
  2. Investigate workload, career pathways, and employee feedback in the Sales Representative and Laboratory Technician roles before selecting role-specific interventions.

Limitations¶

This is a static sample with 237 attrition cases. It does not establish causes or show changes over time, and findings may not generalize to other organizations. The dataset does not specify the currency unit for monthly income.

department_counts = df['Department'].value_counts()

plt.figure(figsize=(10, 6))

department_counts.plot(
    kind='bar',
    color=colors[:len(department_counts)]
)

plt.title("Number of Employees by Department", fontsize=15)
plt.xlabel("Department")
plt.ylabel("Number of Employees")
plt.xticks(rotation=0)
plt.legend(["Employees"])

plt.tight_layout()

plt.savefig("reports/figures/01_bar_chart.png")
plt.show()

Insight:

The bar chart shows the number of employees in each department. It helps identify which department has the highest and lowest employee count.
